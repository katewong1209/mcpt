A rigorous statistical framework to detect data-mining bias and overfitting in algorithmic trading strategies. By destroying serial dependence while preserving marginal distributions and intrabar geometry, MCPT establishes a robust null hypothesis to evaluate whether a strategy's edge is genuine or a product of chance.

## 📐 Methodology & Mathematical Formulation

Standard backtests suffer from path-dependency bias. MCPT addresses this by generating synthetic price paths that preserve the "shape" of the market but destroy its memory.

### 1. Bar Permutation Engine
For a given asset, we transform prices to log-space: $p_t = \ln(C_t)$. We decompose each bar into:
- **Intrabar Geometry**: $\Delta h_t = H_t - O_t$, $\Delta l_t = L_t - O_t$, $\Delta c_t = C_t - O_t$
- **Close-to-Open Gap**: $g_t = O_t - C_{t-1}$

We jointly shuffle the intrabar geometry vectors and separately shuffle the gap vector for $t \in [T_{start}, T_{end}]$. 
**Preserved**: Mean, variance, skewness, kurtosis, intrabar shape, gap distribution, and contemporaneous cross-market correlation (under joint permutation).  
**Destroyed**: Volatility clustering, long memory, self-similarity, and spectral structure.

### 2. Statistical Significance (P-Value)
The strategy is optimized and evaluated on the real data to obtain a baseline Profit Factor ($PF_{real}$). The entire pipeline (including optimization) is then re-run on $N$ permuted datasets. The p-value is calculated with the standard small-sample correction:

$$ p = \frac{1 + \sum_{i=1}^{N} \mathbb{I}(PF_{perm}^{(i)} \ge PF_{real})}{N + 1} $$

A low p-value ($p < 0.05$) indicates the strategy's performance is unlikely to be generated by a random walk with the same marginal properties.

## 🚀 Original Research: Multi-Asset Slippage Testing
*To demonstrate real-world robustness, this repository has been extended to evaluate execution slippage across liquidity regimes.* 

Rather than testing raw mid-price returns, the MCPT engine now applies a **volume-share proportional market impact model**. This allows us to test the hypothesis: *"Does the strategy's edge survive realistic execution costs in illiquid assets, or is it purely a mid-price artifact?"*

See `slippage_mcpt.py` for the implementation comparing liquid vs. illiquid equity execution.

## 📊 Visuals & Key Takeaways

### 1. Permutation Envelope Curve
The app renders the real equity curve overlaid on a shaded "cloud" representing the 5th to 95th percentile of 1,000 permuted equity curves.
- **Takeaway**: If the real strategy's equity curve consistently breaks above the 95th percentile of the permuted null distribution, it confirms the edge is not a result of random volatility clustering.

### 2. Slippage Sensitivity Curve
- **Takeaway**: While the in-sample Profit Factor may appear strong, introducing a realistic 5bps slippage + volume impact model often degrades the illiquid asset's PF significantly, while the liquid asset retains its edge. This proves the strategy is only viable in high-liquidity regimes.
